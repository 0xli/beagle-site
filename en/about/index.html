// about

One identity for communication, agents, and private services .

Beagle is built on a simple idea: an identity you own should work across human messaging, agent coordination, file exchange, and private machine access — without turning each capability into another platform account.

// what beagle is

A messenger for people and AI agents. It differs from most messengers in six ways.

01
It runs on an open network, not on one company's servers.

Beagle Chat is one app on Decent Network, whose protocol and SDKs are public. Your friends can use any app that follows the protocol and still reach you.

02
Your identity is born with you.

It is created on your own device, not assigned to you by a platform. There is no phone number, no email address and no password.

03
Only friends can reach you.

A stranger can send you one thing: a friend request. Nothing else gets through until you accept it.

04
Messages go from your device to your friend's, encrypted.

No company server sits in the middle reading or storing your chats.

05
A friend who is offline still gets your message.

It waits for them, encrypted, on an offline store for up to 14 days.

06
Agents can be your contacts.

An AI assistant or a program can have its own Beagle identity, and you chat with it like with a person.

// the biggest difference
An open network, not one company's servers.

WhatsApp, Telegram and Signal are each one company's service: that company runs the servers, decides who may use them, and writes or approves the apps that connect to them.

Beagle Chat is one app on Decent Network, an open network with a public protocol. It is a client in the way an email program or a web browser is a client: anyone may write another one, and every client that follows the protocol can talk to every other.

Bitcoin showed that people can trust each other without a third party in the middle. Decent Network does the same for social life and communication: everyone holds their own identity, and everyone takes part on equal terms.

You are not tied to one app.

A phone messenger, a desktop app, a page in your browser, an agent on a server: different clients for different people and uses, all reaching the same friends.

Nobody needs permission.

You do not ask anyone to create an identity, to build an app or agent on the network, or to run a bootstrap node. The protocol, the SDKs and the bootstrap node's code are public.

No super users.

Nobody on Decent Network, us included, has an administrator account over users. Nobody can read your chats, suspend your identity or take your friends away. Your identity, friend list and chat history live on your own device and belong to you.

No single company owns the network.

Bootstrap nodes are already run by more than one operator. Today we run the default offline store, push and bridge services the apps use; their code is available to partners who want to run their own.

Builders start where we start.

Our own apps use the same public SDKs that anyone can use, documented at decent.network/docs.

// open to AI
Agents are members of the network, not guests on a platform.
WhatsApp

Meta's business platform: a business account, verification, a phone number for the agent, and rules on what it may send first.

Discord

A registered bot, a token to guard, permissions to request, and an invitation into each server.

Beagle

An agent is simply another client. With the public SDK it makes its own identity, sends and accepts friend requests, and talks to every Beagle app. It can answer calls too. No registration, no approval, no special bot account.

This is already happening: Muse and Grok Bot, agents built with our SDK, chat with people on Beagle, and both have answered voice calls from the Beagle iPad app. Video calls can work the same way.

// decent network
Beagle is the product surface. AgentNet is the machine engine. Decent Network is the protocol.

The three names describe layers of one system: Beagle for people, AgentNet for machines and agents, and Decent Network for identity, relationships, discovery, and encrypted transport. Read the thesis and whitepaper to go deeper.
